\chapter{Literature Review}

The simulation of thermal energy transport in irradiated fuels and fission products is directly correlated with the prediction of reactor behavior. Hurley et al names the problem of understanding thermal transport in nuclear fuel as a grand challenge for both computational and experimental research \autocite{hurleyThermalEnergyTransport2022}. This review paper covers advances in thermal energy transport in actinide oxide fuels from single-crystal simulation to mesoscale thermal transport with irradiation defects. Published in 2022, it is not the most up-to-date review of thermal transport research in nuclear fuels, but it is a good introduction to the subject.

One well documented method for the simulation of heat transport is the Green-Kubo approach for thermal conductivity in solids. In Carbogno et al \autocite{carbognoInitioGreenKuboApproach2017}, they outline the basis for this near-equilibrium method (discussed in chapter \ref{chap:background}) to simulate thermodynamic behavior and account for anharmonic methods with \gls{aimd}. This is a method that I have tried to imitate, and found to be useful. They found that this method is limited by computational expense for adequate sumulation time scales, and that extrapolation techniques can decrease this cost by several orders of magnitude. This method works for harmonic and anharmonic materials at high temperatures on moderate time and length scales.

Uncertainty handling is an important topic for nuclear materials simulation, motivated by the need for high accuracy for cost and safety reasons. It has been argue that rather than trying to identify a perfectly accurate, all-inclusive mechanistic simulation model, it is sufficient to use \gls{uq} with advanced mechanistic models to create a great impact on the time required for \gls{afq} \autocite{matthewsMechanisticMultiscaleUncertainty2026}. In this paper from Matthews et al, they emphasize key strengths of mechanistic modeling: the ability to relate fuel properties via advanced models and data from separate-effect tests to reduce fuel performance uncertainty, giving a flexibility that is not normally available in traditional experiment and development. The key claim is that models do not need to be perfect in order to be useful so long as uncertainties are included in predictions. Through their demonstrated methods, they show that the \gls{mmuq} process allows uncertainty to be propagated from lower-length-scale data to engineering scale simulation, resulting in uncertainty bands tighter than previously predicted.

They leverage empirical models, which require higher safety margins with correspoding additional constraints on power plants, but show an increase in quality when paired with the \gls{ml}-driven refinement techniques and statistical analysis. Key point of this paper is that advanced fuels are limited by the set of experimental data, and so they develop an \gls{afq} methodology that integrates advanced modeling (empirical and cluster dynamics), \gls{ml} techniques, legacy experimental data (the limited data set), and a select set of new experiments to investigate potential failure mechanisms in \gls{un} fuel (paraphrased from conclusions pg 751) \autocite{millerFirstResultsUranium2026}. Their findings provided evidence that multiscale \gls{uq}, facilitated by \gls{ml}, can effectively narrow the safe performance envelope for \gls{un} fuel for \gls{afq}.

An industry-funded perspective on the \gls{afq} intiative comes from General Atomics Electromagnetic Systems posted in the American Nuclear Society Journal of Nuclear Technology in January 2026 \autocite{choiAcceleratedFuelQualification2026}. They announce results from their \gls{afq} of a General Atomics Fast Modular Reactor using paired \gls{ms} with irradiation testing. At a high level, their method is to leverage available experimental data and fast-timescale new experiments to build empirical and assess physics-based mechanistic models, then do coordinated experiments to check and quantify uncertainties within the generated models. They can then redo the \gls{ms} with the updated models to get high quality results with \gls{uq}.

This brief overview of recent literature in thermal transport simulation and advanced fuel qualification methods demonstrate the high interest in advanced \gls{ms} methods in recent years, and how this work serves to add to the available methods for increased-accuracy multiscale methods. By building on previous works \autocite{hurleyThermalEnergyTransport2022,carbognoInitioGreenKuboApproach2017}, and learning about the frameworks used by contempary advanced simulation research for leveraging experiment and reducing uncertainty \autocite{matthewsMechanisticMultiscaleUncertainty2026,millerFirstResultsUranium2026,choiAcceleratedFuelQualification2026}, I can better understand where my research fits into the bigger picture and how to best contribute to future development.
