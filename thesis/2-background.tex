\section{Thermodynamics}
Leading in from the phonon section, give a basis for some of the math theory that I build off of. This might be better located in the computational methods section

According to the 0th law of thermodynamics, any temperature gradient \gls{gradient}\gls{temperature} will spontaneously induce a heat flux \gls{heat flux} to return the system to thermodynamic equilibrium. 

Fourier's Law describes the relationship between the heat flux \gls{heat flux} and the temperature- and pressure- dependent thermal conductivity \gls{kappa}$($\gls{temperature}$,$\gls{pressure}$)$
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{J} = -\boldsymbol{\kappa}(T,p)\cdot \nabla T
\end{equation}

The specific heat capacity (\gls{specific heat capacity}) is defined as the amount of heat that must be supplied to a mass unit of a substance to raise its temperature by some amount. In general, the heat capacity of a substance under a temperature differential (dT) with some mass (m) and supplied heat (dQ) can be shown as:
\begin{equation}
    c=\frac{dQ}{mdT}
\end{equation}
In the more specific case of phonon simulation, specific heat capacity can be defined in terms of the phonon frequency ($\omega$) ELEPHANT ****


\subsection{Green Kubo Theory}
The paper Carbogno et al \autocite{carbognoInitioGreenKuboApproach2017} presents a method for the first-principles formulation of the Green-Kubo method for phonon thermal conductivity of solids in equilibrium. 

The Green Kubo formula for heat flux developed for LAMMPS using the stress (\gls{stress}) of a system of i atoms \autocite{surblysApplicationAtomicStress2019}.
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{J}=\frac{1}{V}\left[ \sum_i E_i * \vec{v}_i - \sum_{i}\boldsymbol{S} \cdot\vec{v}_i \right]
\end{equation}
Which sums the convective flux $J_{conv}=E_i * \vec{v}_i$ and the conductive flux $J_{cond}=-\boldsymbol{S} \cdot\vec{v}_i$.
